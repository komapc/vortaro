A free two-way Ido–Esperanto dictionary

Ido is an international auxiliary language published in 1907 as a reformed Esperanto: it keeps Esperanto's regular grammar, makes the accusative mostly optional and writes without diacritics (Esperanto ĉ, ĝ, ŝ, ŭ…). Esperanto, created by L. L. Zamenhof in 1887, is the most widely spoken planned language. Because the two share most of their roots and affixes, an Ido–Esperanto dictionary is mostly about the small differences in spelling, endings and word choice.

Vortaro lists 37,834 Ido words with their Esperanto translations and 27,605 Esperanto words with their Ido equivalents, built from Ido and Esperanto Wiktionary and from Wikipedia/Wikidata links. Each word has its own page with the part of speech, the regular Ido forms (plural, verb tenses, participles) and related words of the same root. Typing an inflected Ido word such as amas or urbi in the search box finds its dictionary form (amar, urbo). Search works offline once the page has loaded.
